04 / How I Think

How I Think
About Products

Hover any card to see the reasoning behind the principle.

01

Don't give me the solution.

Give me the problem.

"Users want a faster search" is a feature request.
"Users give up before finding what they need" is a problem.
Only the second one leads somewhere worth building.

02

Challenge the obvious.

"Users asked for it" is not evidence.

Users describe symptoms. The PM investigates the underlying problem.
If you build what users ask for, you're a feature factory.
If you build what they actually need, you're building a product.
And if you can't explain it to a non-technical user, you probably don't understand it yet.

03

Name the trade-off.

Speed vs quality. Growth vs retention. Accuracy vs latency.

The worst decisions are where nobody said "we are choosing X over Y."
In AI products: cost / latency / accuracy is a permanent triangle — not a checklist. Pick two deliberately.
Hallucination isn't just an engineering problem. It's a product risk that needs a mitigation strategy at the spec stage.

04

Ship something.

A perfect PRD that never ships is beautifully formatted fiction.

I'd rather own a shipped v1 with known limitations
than a polished v0.9 that never left Notion.
More features ≠ better product. Fewer sharp ones, shipped well, do more.
The product is the experiment. Ship it, measure it, iterate.

05

Measure reality.

If the metric didn't move, the idea didn't work.

Not "we shipped it." Not "users liked it in testing."
The metric. Did it move? By how much? Compared to what?
Data should challenge my opinion, not decorate my presentation.
That's how we know. Then we go again.
