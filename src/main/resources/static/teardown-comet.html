Product Teardown

Comet — From "Which Sneaker?" to "Which Comet Is Me?"

A product discovery teardown: reducing decision friction without taking away the brand's sense of discovery.

01

Problem: "Which sneaker is right for me?"

Comet already has a "Find Your Silhouette" experience covering X Lows, Aeres, Aeon v2, ASTRA, and Apex, with attributes such as cushioning, styling, weight, and fit. The foundation exists. Here's how I would make the discovery journey more decision-oriented.

02

What I observed

  • Comet offers multiple silhouettes.
  • Its Silhouette Finder already compares cushioning, styling, weight and fit.
  • The site provides size selection and product-level information.
  • Customer testimonials repeatedly discuss comfort, fit and everyday use.
03

Friction

Hypothesis Users are given product attributes, but the translation from attribute → personal need → product choice still requires cognitive work. First-time customers may still struggle to translate those attributes into a personal choice.
04

Solution: Silhouette Finder 2.0

A use-case-first Silhouette Finder: ask 3–4 high-signal questions, then return a ranked recommendation with a short “Why this pair” explanation.

  • Q1. What will you use them for? Everyday, Long walks/travel, Going out, Style-first.
  • Q2. How do you like your fit? Roomy, Balanced, Snug.
  • Q3. What's your style? Minimal, Street, Retro, Runner.
  • Q4. What matters most? Comfort, Weight, Style, Versatility.
05

Recommendation & Trade-off

More guidance vs less discovery. Too much recommendation can make Comet feel like a generic footwear catalogue. Too little guidance creates decision friction. So, don't recommend one shoe immediately. Recommend 2–3 candidates and explain the trade-off.

Your Match 1. X LOWS — Best for: Street + everyday wear, Roomier fit.
2. AERES — Best for: Lightweight everyday wear, Narrower fit.
3. APEX — Best for: More cushioning, Relaxed casual styling.
06

Metrics

North Star / Primary Finder-assisted add-to-cart rate
SecondaryFinder completion rate, Recommendation → PDP CTR, PDP → cart conversion, Cart → purchase conversion, Size/fit exchange rate, 30/90-day repeat purchase
GuardrailsReturn rate, Customer support contacts, Recommendation abandonment
07

Experiment

A/B test:

  • Control: Current Find Your Silhouette.
  • Variant: Use-case-first recommendation flow.
  • Measure: Add-to-cart conversion among first-time users who interact with the tool.

Success would require a statistically credible improvement in the primary metric without deterioration in return/exchange rate. If users can translate their own needs into a recommended silhouette earlier, Comet may reduce decision friction and improve downstream conversion without changing the catalogue itself.

08

What I wouldn't build

Because the first problem is decision clarity, not lack of AI. I would not start with an ML recommendation engine. The first version can be rule-based because the product catalogue already contains structured attributes.

  • Build an LLM chatbot immediately
  • Build a computer-vision foot scanner immediately
  • Replace the existing catalogue
  • Recommend based only on purchase history
  • Optimize solely for conversion
This is an independent product teardown based on publicly observable product flows. Internal funnel, conversion and return data were not available; proposed friction points are hypotheses to validate.
